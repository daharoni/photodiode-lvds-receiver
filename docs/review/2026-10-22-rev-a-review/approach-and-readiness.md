## Executive summary

1. I found no BLOCKER inside my scope: the analog-first architecture is coherent, the HV-off-by-default gate is fail-safe by construction, and the USB-C foreign-host analysis is careful. The board can be ordered as an experiment board; the open question is whether the OPTICAL LINK closes, which this board cannot answer by itself.
2. MAJOR: the link-budget headline ("APD + a modestly brighter NIR emitter closes the link", decision 8) rests on an energy-conservation error in the APD sweep (30 mW/sr over a 140 degree beam "from 25 mW average electrical" implies 229 % wall-plug efficiency) and on a 35 MHz noise bandwidth where the as-built chain has about 70 MHz. My redo: Q = 6 at 35 cm lateral needs about 6-12 mW/sr (23-46 mW optical flux), i.e. roughly 40-180 mW average electrical per emitter against the stated ~200 mW whole-headstage budget, with the best APD gain at M about 86-114 (at or above the S12023-10 M max of 100), not M 35-60.
3. MAJOR: the "39 dB single-setting + 32 dB firmware" range figures are electrical (20 log I) dB. In optical power they are about 16-25 dB (fixed setting) and about 16 dB (firmware). Firmware range control only extends toward STRONG light; at the weak end there is no knob left, so fade margin equals emitter margin.
4. MAJOR (spec consistency): "module decodes with the ESP32 unprogrammed" (CLAUDE.md hard rule 1, decision 12, architecture) is false as built unless the DNP bench jumper J1 is fitted: an unprogrammed ESP32 means HV_DIS is pulled high, so the APD bias is OFF and there is no signal. HV safety design is right; the claim needs rewording and a gate item.
5. Readiness: ERC clean and routing complete, but G4 pre-order items are only partly done (JLCPCB impedance calculator not run, 52 DRC items not dispositioned in docs, no FPGA-end breakout board, no defined G1 optical test source, no enclosure), and four authority/derived docs still say "nothing is routed".

## Findings

### MAJOR

**A1. Link-budget emitter power is not physically consistent; the APD "closes the link" claim is overstated.**
- Location: docs/calculations/link-budget.md, APD sweep Table B and "What this means" item 4; decision-log entry 8; requirements row 2 ("modestly brighter NIR emitter").
- Saw: "S12023-10 30 mW/sr 140deg ... I = 33.3 mA, Pavg 25.0 mW" with the footnote "targets at 0.9 mW/sr/mA". 0.9 mW/sr/mA is the SFH 4715AS figure (+-45 degree class, 900 mW/sr at 1 A, link-budget.md assumptions table), applied here to a 140 degree beam.
- Why it matters (PROPOSED arithmetic, lb.py): flux of a cos^m beam = Ie x 2 pi/(m+1), m = 0.646 (the doc's own value for 140 degrees). 30 mW/sr gives 115 mW optical flux; the doc's drive is 33.3 mA x 1.5 V = 50 mW peak electrical, so the implied wall-plug efficiency is 229 %. The only 140 degree emitter datasheet in the doc (QBLP601-2IR4, 1.0 mW/sr at 20 mA, 1.5 V assumed) gives flux 3.8 mW from 30 mW, i.e. 12.7 % efficiency, versus the 30 % "WPE ASSUMED (optimistic)" in the same doc. At 12.7-30 % efficiency the 30 mW/sr case costs 190-450 mW average electrical, not 25 mW. Also 30 mW/sr is 12x (10.8 dB) the reference QBLP601 at 50 mA (2.5 mW/sr), which is not "modest".
- My Q=6 requirement (see link-budget table below): 11.5-12 mW/sr with the as-built 70 MHz noise bandwidth (5.9 mW/sr with the doc's 35 MHz), flux 44-46 mW (23 mW), average electrical at 50 % duty 73-76 mW at 30 % efficiency or 169-176 mW at 13 % (38 / 88 mW for the 35 MHz case). The stated headstage budget is "about 200 mW average for the whole headstage (~500 mW wireless power)" (link-budget.md). So closure at 35 cm lateral looks marginal to infeasible with an LED of QBLP601-class efficiency unless the coverage target, optics (etendue allows 2x at +-45 degrees), or receiver count changes.
- Fix: correct Table B (derive current from flux and a measured/datasheet efficiency, not from mW/sr/mA of a different part); restate decision 8's consequence as "needs ~10x the reference emitter intensity, ~45 mW optical flux at 35 cm lateral; headstage power feasibility UNRESOLVED"; get Daniel's emitter co-design numbers before claiming closure. Do not change decision 8 (APD still the right direction: the PIN needs 129-254 mW/sr, VERIFIED arithmetic in the doc), but the "modestly brighter" wording should go.
- Confidence: HIGH on the inconsistency; MED on my absolute thresholds (they depend on R = 0.47 A/W graph read, 0.9 transmission, ambient 0, and the flat-density noise scaling).

**A2. Noise bandwidth and optimum M in the budget do not match the built chain.**
- Location: link-budget.md method item 5 (B = 35 MHz, "ASSUMED"), architecture.md signal-chain item 6 ("Q(M) peaks at M 35-60"), apd-bias-design.md.
- Saw: TIA noise is scaled as 15 nA x sqrt(35/135) = 7.64 nA (low bound), and Q is computed for B = 35 MHz (0.7 x bit rate). As built: OPA857 -3 dB 99-106 MHz (SIMULATED 99 MHz at 6 pF; datasheet 105 MHz at 1.5 pF, SBOS630D p.6, VERIFIED), then a 68 MHz low-pass (R56 49.9 / C54 47 pF), so the noise equivalent bandwidth is about 65-75 MHz, not 35 MHz. No matched or 35 MHz filter exists in the chain.
- Why it matters: TIA noise rises by sqrt(2) = 1.4x (flat-density assumption, which the doc itself calls a lower bound because the e_n x C term rises with frequency; at 6 pF the datasheet-plot read is 26 nA at 135 MHz). That is about 1.5 dB optical. It also moves the Q-optimal M up: my redo finds Q-max at M = 86-106 for the 12 mW/sr case (M = 92-149 at lower signal), versus the doc's M 35-62 (30 mW/sr case) and M 93 for the QBLP601 case. The S12023-10 datasheet M max is 100 (link-budget assumptions table). Operating near M max means the bias tempco matters most (about 7 %/V of gain, calc doc 3.2; residual +-0.7 V over 15-35 C gives +-5 % gain), the per-unit VBR spread (150 typ / 200 max) matters most, and the firmware "trim toward the Q optimum" has almost no upward headroom.
- Fix: rerun `tools/link_budget.py` with B about 70 MHz and the 6 pF noise column (the doc says "not scripted"); update the Q(M) optimum statement in architecture.md; consider whether a 35-40 MHz filter in place of 68 MHz (R56/C54 swap, both 0402-class parts, no layout change) buys back the 1.5 dB at the cost of ISI (PROPOSED, trade needs the G1 eye).
- Confidence: HIGH on the bandwidth mismatch; MED on the size of the effect.

**A3. "39 dB / 32 dB" ranges are electrical dB; optical ranges are half as large, and range control only extends the strong end.**
- Location: decision-log 12, architecture.md item 6, post-amp-slicer.md section 3, gates G2 ("~39 dB single-setting window").
- Saw: "63-126x (36-42 dB)" is a current ratio (20 log). Photocurrent is proportional to optical power, so optical dB = half. PROPOSED, lb.py: Q = 6 at the OPA857 20k stage needs Ipp of about 0.22-0.29 uA (12 x 18.7-24.3 nA); the post-amp clip is 7.9-9.0 uA at 20k (1.0-1.15 Vpp over 7 x 18.2 k; datasheet VOL 1.05/1.15 V and VOH 2.35 V, SBOS622D p.6-7, VERIFIED, gives only +0.5 V low-side headroom from a 1.65 V baseline, so the doc's 9.0 uA is the optimistic end); the TIA itself saturates at 60 uA (OPA857 p.15, VERIFIED in frontend.md). So the clean-linear window is 31-32 dB electrical = about 15.5 dB optical, and the clipped-but-slicing window to 60 uA is about 49 dB electrical = about 24.5 dB optical. Firmware adds M 50 to 5 (20 dB electrical = 10 dB optical) and 20k to 5k (12 dB electrical = 6 dB optical): 16 dB optical, not 32.
- Why it matters: (a) the expected arena spread (about 3 dB optical lateral, 6 dB per halving of height, plus orientation, shadowing and tilt, which are unmeasured) is compared in the doc to the 30-36 dB figure as if optical; (b) every firmware knob lowers gain. Fade-in (weak light) has NO remedy: M is already near its maximum at the Q=6 point (A2), 20k is already the high-gain mode. The link's fade margin is therefore exactly the emitter margin at the receiver, which A1 says is the thin part.
- Fix: restate ranges in optical dB with both unit conventions; add an explicit "weak-end margin" row to the budget (dB between operating point and Q=3.09 / Q=6 floor).
- Confidence: HIGH (units), MED (clip levels until G1 clip test).

**A4. "Module decodes with the ESP32 unprogrammed" is not true without J1; no gate tests the analog-only mode.**
- Location: CLAUDE.md Purpose and hard rule 1; architecture.md signal chain item 6 ("With the ESP32 unprogrammed the module runs at the resistor-set bias and 20k") and state table (POWER-UP "ESP32 verifies DAC EEPROM, enables HV"); docs/schematic/power.md "HV disable logic".
- Saw: power.md table: "HV_DIS high, or ESP32 unprogrammed/Hi-Z (R33 pulls the gate to 3V3_D) -> Q3 on -> EN/UVLO pulled to GND -> HV OFF". J1 "HV FORCE ON" is DNP and described as "bench only". architecture.md item 11 itself says "HV stays off until HV_DIS is driven low or the J1 bench jumper is fitted". So the same document set says both things.
- Why it matters: the firm user rule (analog-first) is that slicing does not depend on firmware. As built, the APD has no bias, hence no signal, until firmware (or J1) enables it. That is the correct safety choice (HV off by default), but then the rule should read "with J1 fitted the module slices with no firmware", and a gate should prove it (blank ESP32, J1 fitted, DAC default/Hi-Z gives the 136 V resistor-set bias per calc doc 3.2, TIA 20k default, LVDS valid). Note also that with J1 fitted HV starts at the DAC pin default with no ramp and no firmware fault supervision (a faulted HV start can trip a USB port, decision 13): bench only.
- Fix: reword in CLAUDE.md/architecture/decision 12; add a G1 item "ESP32 blank, J1 fitted: decode with HV from the resistor-set bias"; fit J1 on first-article boards (a 2-pin header, no layout change).
- Confidence: HIGH.

**A5. No single interface specification between the headstage transmitter and this receiver; AC-coupling imposes a transmitter obligation that is not written down.**
- Location: requirements.md row 1 ("continuous stream with regular commas", ASSUMED, open item 3), firmware/ecp5/README.md section 3 (the only definition), frontend.md (C51 100 nF/R51 1 k and C55 100 nF/R59 1 k).
- Saw: the gateware README defines the line protocol (8b/10b clause 36, idle K28.5 D16.2, K27.7/K29.7 framing, CRC-16, 262-byte max frame = 52.4 us; VERIFIED as an executable model with 257 passing sim tests, README s.0), but it labels it "PROPOSED; the headstage transmitter must match". There is no jitter/eye budget document anywhere (grep for jitter budget / eye mask / jitter tolerance finds nothing outside the gateware tests, which use 0.2-0.3 UI injected jitter).
- Why it matters (PROPOSED): both coupling stages are 100 us (1.6 kHz). 8b/10b DC balance keeps wander negligible in running data (bounded disparity, 100 ns / 100 us = 0.1 % for a 5-UI run), but any transmitter silence longer than a few microseconds (inter-frame gaps, sleep/power-saving, LED brownout) lets the baseline relax to the "light off" level; on resumption the data sits offset by about half the amplitude until both stages re-settle (about 3 tau each, order 0.3-0.6 ms). The 20 ms reacquisition goal tolerates that once, but not repeated gapping. The transmitter must therefore (a) send idle commas continuously, (b) never go dark, (c) keep the LED average current constant in idle, and (d) have rise/fall times compatible with 20 ns UI: SFH 4550 is 12 ns tr/tf and SFH 4715AS 11/14 ns (link-budget.md), and the Wurth red part has no speed spec (requirements), so the emitter alone can consume most of the eye. The link budget includes no LED-bandwidth/ISI penalty at all.
- The CDR is a blind 4x oversampler at 200 MHz (5 ns pitch, 0.25 UI): the decision itself is quantised to +-0.125 UI before any analog impairment. Analog impairments at the sensitivity floor (PROPOSED arithmetic from post-amp-slicer.md numbers): comparator offset +-5 mV max over a slew of about 2.8 mV/ns (28 mVpp, 5.8 ns 10-90) shifts each crossing +-1.8 ns (up to about 18 % UI duty distortion), plus the simulated 13.8 % overshoot / 12.6 ns settle of the 20k TIA at 6 pF, plus LED rise time. No one has summed these against the CDR tolerance.
- Fix: write one "optical link interface" page (continuous idle, comma, max run, frame length, rise/fall limits, minimum eye at the comparator, DC-balance obligations, duty/DC content of idle), reference it from requirements.md and the headstage design; add a short jitter/eye budget table with the CDR quantisation.
- Confidence: HIGH that the document is missing; MED on the severity numbers.

### MINOR (still worth acting on before the order if cheap)

**B1. Operating-point sensitivity floor ignores comparator offset and noise.** The 28-37 mVpp at the comparator at Q = 6 (12 sigma, sigma = 2.4-3.1 mV rms at the post-amp output: 18.7-24.3 nA x 18.2 k x 7, PROPOSED) can be eroded by the TLV3605 offset of +-5 mV max (VERIFIED, TLV3605 SNOSDA2F p.7 per post-amp-slicer.md): worst case about 2.6 dB electrical (1.3 dB optical) to hold Q = 6. The 3.0 mV external hysteresis is about 1.0-1.3 sigma of the noise at the comparator, so it is not a noise-rejection element; at dark it will not stop chatter (the CDR/lane_rx lock rule of 128 valid symbols makes that harmless: random data cannot hold 128 valid symbols). PROPOSED; G1 "Comparator small-signal test" covers it. Add "offset after DAC_TH trim" and "chatter in dark" explicitly to that gate item. Confidence MED.

**B2. HV down-slew is slow, and the default 20k mode is the worst case for a strong-light event.** apd-bias-design.md section 4-5: after disable or a lower set point the supply only bleeds through the divider, tau about 0.37 s (220 V to 60 V in 0.49 s); the LT8365 in pulse-skip cannot sink. So lowering M after a flash takes 50-200 ms, not "tens of ms". The hardware bound is good: the series current clamp (about 150 uA, DER) plus the 37 k filter drop (3.7-5.5 V at 100-150 uA, about 30-40 % M reduction at M about 100) limit APD current to below the 5k saturation of 240 uA. But in the 20k default the TIA saturates at 60 uA, so between 60 and 150 uA the front end is overloaded until firmware flips CTRL to 5k. The G1 clip test is specified only to "2x and 10x over the 9 uA (20k) clip point" (18 and 90 uA), i.e. it does not reach 150 uA. Add a 150 uA overload/recovery step at 20k and 5k to G1. Loop speed itself is not a problem: ADS1115 is 860 SPS max (about 1.2 ms per conversion, three channels multiplexed gives about 4 ms per full set; I read this from my knowledge of the part, not re-checked against docs/datasheets/ads1115.pdf), MCP4728 and I2C at 400 kHz are far faster than the plant, so the loop is plant-limited (HV settling 2.8 ms to 1 % on the filter plus the divider bleed), not ESP32-limited. UNRESOLVED: maximum fade rate (requirements open item 2). Confidence MED.

### QUESTIONS / UNRESOLVED (decisions stand; these are for Daniel)

**Q1. Default TIA gain with an unprogrammed ESP32 is 20k.** Simulation (not measured) shows 13.8 % overshoot and 12.6 ns settle at 6 pF in 20k (docs/simulation/README.md Sim 2), versus a clean 5k, and 20k saturates at 60 uA while the clamp allows 150 uA. Trade: 5k has 1.7x the input noise (44 vs 26 nA at 6 pF, read from plot). I would keep 20k until the G1 eye measurement says otherwise, but flag that the analog-only mode (A4) inherits the worse ISI. Is a JP10-style jumper default worth choosing after G1?

**Q2. Fixed x7 post-amp (decision 12): what is the fallback if the real clip level or noise is wrong?** The OPA855 minimum stable gain is 7 (datasheet), so gain can only go up (swap R53/R54, 0402-class), never down; there is no attenuator or bypass footprint ahead of the post-amp, and the clip headroom is +0.5 V low side from 1.65 V at VOL max 1.15 V (A3). Fallbacks on the board today: raise gain by resistor swap (weak-signal side), change LPF R56/C54 (noise/ISI trade), change R87/C87 (hysteresis), change coupling RC, 5k default. To REDUCE gain you would need a respin or a hand-soldered series/shunt pad. Consider whether Rev A needs a series-R footprint at the PA input. Not a blocker.

**Q3. Is the S12023-10 the right detector if the best M is 86-114?** At M near the datasheet maximum (100) tempco and VBR spread dominate; decision 8 stands, but you may want G1 to record M(V, T) up to the breakdown knee and the achievable Q, before the headstage emitter is frozen. (S12023-05 has less capacitance but a quarter of the area, link-budget Table A.)

**Q4. Does the headstage power budget allow the emitter A1 implies (about 45 mW optical flux)?** Only Daniel/the headstage design can answer; this determines whether a larger collection aperture, a narrower coverage target, or more receivers per arena is needed.

Estimates versus measurements: everything optical is an estimate. Received power at the receiver has never been measured (CLAUDE.md, requirements row 2); emitter intensity is datasheet typ at 20 mA scaled linearly (the doc itself says optimistic: droop, heating); R at 765 nm is a graph read; the OPA857 6 pF noise is read by eye from a plot; the APD excess-noise exponent is the datasheet-typical 0.3; the 1 mm die area collected without optics.

**Single biggest uncertainty: the optical signal actually delivered to the APD (emitter radiant intensity and beam shape at the real geometry, through the real bandpass filter and window, with the real ambient).** It sets Q by a factor of 10x or more (A1), it dominates the two next-largest unknowns (TIA noise at 6 pF, comparator small-signal behaviour) which are G1 board measurements, and it needs no Rev A board to measure. First measurement to close it: put the candidate headstage emitter (or the existing QBLP601/Wurth part at the real current) at the paper's geometry (50 cm height, 0, 20 and 35 cm lateral), with the real filter and window over a calibrated large-area silicon photodiode or the APD at M = 1 (a few volts bias), and read the DC (or 1 kHz chopped, lock-in) photocurrent with a picoammeter. A DC/lock-in photocurrent measurement needs no 50 MHz electronics. Then repeat with the emitter off to log the in-band ambient photocurrent in the actual lab. Compare to my 1.6 nA at 2.5 mW/sr, 35 cm lateral (M = 1).

## Dynamic range and range-control plan (item 3 in the brief, consolidated)

- Window: electrically 31-32 dB (linear) to 49 dB (clipped but slicing) at the fixed 20k/M; optically about 15.5 dB and 24.5 dB (A3). Strong end is hardware-bounded: the 150 uA clamp and 37 k filter drop keep APD current below the 5k TIA saturation (240 uA) with M falling 30-40 %; the weak end has no knob (A2/A3).
- Fade: the receiver drops to the sensitivity floor as optical power falls; firmware can only raise M within about 1.3x (M near max). Recovery after a strong event: CTRL flip is fast, M down-slew 50-200 ms (B2). Reacquisition <= 20 ms is a firmware-plus-HV property that depends on the dwell the firmware picks (UNRESOLVED until G3); it can only be met if reacquisition is possible without lowering HV (that is, via 20k to 5k).
- ESP32 + MCP4728 + ADS1115: loop capability about 100+ Hz, more than enough; plant-limited as above. RSSI is a diode peak detector with 1 M x 1 nF (1 ms tau) biased by about 1.5 uA (control.md): its small-signal response (5-25 mV TIA signals) sits on the BAS70 knee, so expect compression at the weak end; G2 "RSSI vs photocurrent monotonic and repeatable" covers it, and the FPGA validity is the real LOS and quality metric (as the docs say).
- Dark start: HV off until firmware enables; firmware starts HV stepped from the 0xFFF code (about 104 V) in USB-alone mode (decision 13). Dark: TIA noise chatters the comparator at up to the noise bandwidth, the 128-valid-symbol lock rule and the "both LVDS constant" LOS rule handle it (PROPOSED). Strong-light start: powers up at the 20k default with M from the resistor-set bias; recovers by CTRL flip (B2).
- Baseline wander: 100 us x 2 AC stages; 8b/10b bounded disparity and K28.5 D16.2 idle are fine in continuous operation (DC content of the idle pair is zero by construction; fundamental 2.5 MHz; run length max 5). The risk is gaps (A5) and clipping asymmetry: low-side headroom 0.5-0.6 V versus high-side 0.7-0.8 V from the baseline at VOL/VOH (VERIFIED limits, derived headroom) so the "clipping is symmetric about the baseline" statement in decision 12 is true to about 0.2 V only; with DC-balanced data an asymmetric clip shifts the post-clip average by a fraction of the clipped amount and the second AC stage then moves the baseline by that fraction (PROPOSED). G1 "clip test with a 5-UI pattern, measure recovery and zero-crossing shift" is the right test; add a long-pattern (PRBS-derived 8b/10b with commas) run at 2x/10x clip with BER, not only edge shift.
- DNP/fallback on board for x7: see Q2.
